### 1. PlayCanvas Relighting of Gaussian Splat Scenes — @Martin Valigursky

**Source:** timeline | **Time:** 2026-06-11 14:46 UTC

Gaussian splats are captures frozen in time — not anymore. Experimental **relighting** of splat scenes in the PlayCanvas engine, driven by a proxy mesh: swap the sky, drag the sun with its soft shadows, drop in point lights — all live on a captured scene. Includes a runnable demo.

This is a significant capability extension for the [[3d-gaussian-splatting]] ecosystem. Until now, splats were static lighting captures — illumination baked into the scene data. Relighting via a proxy mesh means captured scenes become editable: time-of-day changes, artificial lights, compositing-target lighting. PlayCanvas (which also produces [[supersplat]]) is extending its engine from viewing/editing to interactive lighting manipulation. The proxy-mesh approach suggests a lightweight geometry underlying the splats, enabling shadow casting and light interaction without full mesh reconstruction.

**Relevance:** Fresh 3DGS signal extending the PlayCanvas/SuperSplat narrative; relighting is a new capability dimension not previously captured.

### 2. 1 Billion Gaussians at 60fps — @MrNeRF

**Source:** timeline | **Time:** 2026-06-11 11:04 UTC

1 Billion Gaussians (1,035,804,128 exactly) streamed at 60fps vsync in 5GB VRAM. "There is no limit anymore to how much it can do."

A major rendering milestone. 1B Gaussians is ~40× the 24M demonstrated by [[supersplat]] recently. The 5GB VRAM at vsync-locked 60fps implies a streaming or LOD architecture. MrNeRF (maintainer of [[awesome-3dgs]]) is a credible source for 3DGS benchmarks.

**Relevance:** Major 3DGS scaling milestone; extends the performance narrative already tracked in the wiki.
